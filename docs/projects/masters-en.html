Problem

In the Ghasha field offshore Abu Dhabi, salt and basement lie under 8–10 km of sediments, so gravity and magnetic signals overlap; earlier methods put the salt at ~6,000 m, far shallower than the ~9,300 m seen in seismic data

My role

Propose an analysis that separates basement and salt signals in airborne gravity and magnetic data

What I did

  1. Built a wavenumber correlation filtering (WCF) pipeline — upward continuation and normalization, then the correlation coefficient CC(k) between the two signals at each wavenumber
  2. Designed two indices — SLFI from positively correlated components (basement) and DLFI from weak/negative ones (salt)
  3. Chose thresholds from energy–correlation trade-off curves instead of by eye (upward continuation 1,500 m at the knee point; SLFI band 0.5–1.0 keeps ~half the energy at correlation 0.85)
  4. Validated against three independent published models with map, profile and 3-D density comparisons
  5. Presented Abu Dhabi processing and analysis results at 10+ regular lab research meetings (2024.05–2026.01) and at the 2025.11 Basic Research Lab workshop
  6. Researcher on national R&D projects, including the IITP global talent program

Results

  • SLFI basement structure agrees with three independent models at r ≥ 0.94 (profile correlation 0.95–0.98 after shift alignment)
  • DLFI reproduces the salt distribution and suggests an additional salt uplift west of Ghasha
  • First-author paper in SPE Journal (SCIE, 2026, with Univ. of Calgary and Khalifa Univ.) · 3 conference talks (2024)
  • M.S. degree conferred 2026.08.21

Decisions & trade-offs

What I chose, and what I gave up for it

Correlation band: a narrower band raises the correlation but leaves less signal energy, so I plotted correlation and retained-energy ratio together and picked the balance point (SLFI: CC 0.5–1.0, keeping about half the energy) — a threshold that can be explained with a graph, not by feel.
Upward-continuation height: higher reduces shallow noise but blurs resolution; I chose the knee of the correlation-change curve (1,500 m).
Validation reference: a reference built from the same data is not validation, so I compared against three independent models from different methods (map overlay, four profiles, 3D density sections) — harder to obtain, but avoids circular validation.

Looking back · open questions

What I would change, and what is still open

The additional salt uplift west of Ghasha suggested by DLFI remains a hypothesis to confirm with other data such as seismic.
The real data belong to the joint project and stay private; the workflow is reproduced on synthetic data in gravmag-wcf. Applying the same procedure to another region is the next step.
