Problem

Wildfires spread quickly with wind and vegetation, yet Korea had no real-time spread prediction model

My role

Design and train the spatiotemporal model that predicts where the fire will be next

What I did

  1. Input rasters: fire fronts digitized in GIS from Korea Forest Service fire maps, plus weather, NDVI and elevation
  2. Designed a U-LSTM (Conv2D encoder → LSTM → Conv2DTranspose decoder) kept shallow at 2,736 parameters because data were scarce
  3. Expanded the short time series with sliding windows of length 1–19 into 151 training batches
  4. Implemented the model in TensorFlow and PyTorch and evaluated it with Overall Accuracy and F1-score
  5. Input data: fire fronts, elevation, NDVI, wind direction and speed of four large fires (Uljin–Samcheok, Gunwi, Miryang, Gangneung) rasterized in ArcGIS — three for training, one held out to test generalization

Results

  • Unseen fire (Gangneung): Accuracy 0.90 · F1 0.85 (validation 0.95 · 0.81)
  • Refactored the team notebooks into a tested Python package and documented the limitations on GitHub

Decisions & trade-offs

What I chose, and what I gave up for it

Only four fires of data: instead of a bigger model, a shallow network with 2,736 parameters, and sliding windows of length 1–19 to grow the training set to 151 batches.
For a fair generalization check, one entire fire (Gangneung) was held out for testing — less training data, but a real 'unseen fire' result.

Looking back · open questions

What I would change, and what is still open

I didn't trace which axis the tensors actually flowed along; I later realized the LSTM was looking at the spatial axis rather than time.
If I rebuilt it: ConvLSTM, event-level cross-validation, Dice loss + IoU, and satellite-based fire-front data.
